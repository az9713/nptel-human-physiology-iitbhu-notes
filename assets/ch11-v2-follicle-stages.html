Keep these routes distinct

Iodide: interstitium → basolateral uptake → cell → apical efflux → colloid.

Tg scaffold: cell secretory pathway → apical exocytosis → colloid; iodinated Tg returns by endocytosis.

Free T3/T4: Tg proteolysis → intracellular handling → basal export → plasma binding. TBG in plasma is different from follicular Tg.

TSH: binds an extracellular receptor and regulates the pathway; it need not enter through its receptor.

Complete ordered equivalent

Read the source image above together with these stages. The unmarked image is the observed evidence; all transport statements below are physiological interpretation from separate sources.

  1. Basolateral iodide uptake. NIS couples iodide uptake to the inward Na⁺ gradient maintained by Na/K ATPase.
  2. Apical supply. Apical anion transport supplies the follicular lumen. Pendrin is a proposed route, with an unresolved in-vivo contribution in the cited account; it is not an ATPase.
  3. Tg secretion. The ER/Golgi secretory pathway delivers Tg in a vesicle lumen. Apical fusion opens that lumen to colloid, without Tg crossing an intact bilayer.
  4. Assembly and storage. TPO/H₂O₂-dependent iodination and coupling form hormone residues on Tg near the apical–colloid interface. Hormone remains Tg-bound in the luminal store.
  5. Tg retrieval. Endocytosis encloses iodinated Tg in a membrane-bounded intracellular compartment.
  6. Proteolytic release. Endolysosomal processing liberates T3/T4. Iodotyrosine deiodination recycles iodine separately; no unidentified lysosomal membrane transporter is invented.
  7. Basal hormone export. Free hormone is exported toward blood through transport machinery, with MCT8 involvement demonstrated in mouse experiments. Subsequent plasma-carrier binding differs from Tg storage.
