A soluble protein follows a selected secretory route

Follow the highlighted cargo from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), through the Golgi apparatus, to release outside the cell. Use the controls to inspect each stage.

Before fusion, the selected soluble cargo occupies the carrier lumen, separated from cytosol by membrane. Fusion connects the carrier membrane to the plasma membrane and opens the carrier lumen to extracellular fluid. Cargo leaves through this opening; it does not diffuse through the intact lipid bilayer. The remaining carrier membrane becomes part of the cell surface.

The upper panel is an itinerary map, not a construction of closed ER or Golgi lumens. Its marker stops inside the cell; the lower enlargement continues the final step. The lower view previews the final carrier state until the itinerary reaches its last segment; it is not a simultaneous measurement of two compartments. The panels represent the same selected cargo at different levels of explanation, not two cargo cohorts. A closed carrier precedes an idealized finite open pore; this diagram does not resolve hemifusion, pore nucleation, pore expansion, lipid rearrangement or actual molecular kinetics. The omega profile is held open to make membrane continuity inspectable at pause and seek.

Static route for print or without JavaScript: ER lumen → carrier lumen → Golgi lumen → secretory carrier lumen → open fusion pore → extracellular fluid. The carrier's outer surface faces cytosol before fusion; soluble cargo never crosses the intact bilayer in this route.

Mechanism reference: OpenStax, cellular organelles and the endomembrane system. The drawing isolates membrane-side continuity; it does not reproduce measured molecular geometry or fusion kinetics.
