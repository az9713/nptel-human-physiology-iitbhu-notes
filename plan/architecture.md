# Chapter architecture

Scientific dependencies point backward and are distinct from reading progression.

- **1. From cells to organ systems:** lectures[1, 2, 3]; scientific prerequisites[]; Explain how cellular organization supports integrated body function, with anatomical orientation and tissue context.
- **2. Membrane transport and homeostasis:** lectures[4, 5, 6]; scientific prerequisites[1]; Connect selective transport and energy coupling to the maintenance of regulated internal conditions.
- **3. Blood, hemostasis and anticoagulation:** lectures[7, 8, 9]; scientific prerequisites[1, 2]; Explain blood-cell roles and how local hemostasis is initiated, amplified and limited.
- **4. Blood groups, lymph and immune defense:** lectures[10, 11, 12]; scientific prerequisites[1, 3]; Link antigen recognition, component-specific transfusion compatibility and immune surveillance.
- **5. The heart as an electrical and mechanical pump:** lectures[13, 14]; scientific prerequisites[1, 2]; Connect cardiac anatomy, conduction, pressure changes and circuit flow without confusing electrical signals with contraction.
- **6. Blood vessels and microvascular exchange:** lectures[15]; scientific prerequisites[2, 5]; Explain how vessel architecture and local forces control flow, exchange and edema.
- **7. Airways, ventilation and hemoglobin:** lectures[16, 17]; scientific prerequisites[1, 2, 5]; Trace air movement to the alveolar interface and distinguish ventilation, diffusion and hemoglobin binding.
- **8. Oxygen delivery, lung volumes and measurement:** lectures[18, 19]; scientific prerequisites[3, 5, 7]; Quantify oxygen transport and explain why oxygen saturation alone cannot identify every form of impaired oxygen delivery.
- **9. Endocrine signaling and glucose homeostasis:** lectures[20, 21]; scientific prerequisites[1, 2]; Explain endocrine feedback and how insulin/glucagon regulate glucose through target-cell processes.
- **10. Diabetes technologies and experimental cell therapy:** lectures[22, 23]; scientific prerequisites[2, 9]; Separate measurement, insulin delivery, closed-loop control and investigational cell replacement.
- **11. Thyroid and endocrine disorders:** lectures[24, 25]; scientific prerequisites[2, 9]; Connect hormone synthesis and endocrine feedback to the interpretation of dysfunction.
- **12. Digestive organization and carbohydrate absorption:** lectures[26, 27]; scientific prerequisites[1, 2]; Follow a meal through digestion and explain the transition from luminal breakdown to epithelial transport.
- **13. Protein and lipid absorption; digestive dysfunction:** lectures[28, 29, 30]; scientific prerequisites[2, 12]; Compare protein and lipid handling and connect specific failed steps to malabsorption.
- **14. Bones, joints and connective tissues:** lectures[31, 32]; scientific prerequisites[1, 9]; Relate bone and joint structure to mechanical function and tissue maintenance.
- **15. Muscle contraction and neuromuscular transmission:** lectures[33, 34, 35]; scientific prerequisites[1, 2, 14]; Connect neural excitation to calcium regulation and force generation while distinguishing muscle types.
- **16. Kidney organization and glomerular filtration:** lectures[36, 37]; scientific prerequisites[1, 2, 5, 6]; Explain how renal blood supply and filtration barriers generate filtrate without equating blood flow with filtration rate.
- **17. Tubular transport, renal regulation and disease:** lectures[38, 39, 40]; scientific prerequisites[2, 9, 16]; Track filtered substances through the nephron and link segmental transport to water balance and pressure regulation.
- **18. Male reproduction and spermatogenesis:** lectures[41, 42]; scientific prerequisites[1, 9]; Connect reproductive anatomy, gamete development and endocrine regulation.
- **19. Female anatomy, ovarian cycles and lactation:** lectures[43, 44, 45]; scientific prerequisites[1, 9, 18]; Explain follicular development, oocyte maturation and ovulation across distinct biological timescales.
- **20. Menstrual regulation, fertilization and pregnancy:** lectures[46, 47]; scientific prerequisites[9, 19]; Link ovarian/endometrial regulation to fertilization, implantation, pregnancy adaptation, labor and delivery.
- **21. Contraception, assisted reproduction and reproductive health:** lectures[48, 49, 50]; scientific prerequisites[18, 19, 20]; Explain intervention mechanisms and evidence limits while separating contraception, fertility treatment and disease management.
- **22. Nervous-system organization: brain, spinal cord and nerves:** lectures[51, 52, 53]; scientific prerequisites[1, 2]; Build the anatomical organization needed to locate neural pathways and interpret later cellular mechanisms.
- **23. Neurons, axonal transport and nerve repair:** lectures[54, 55]; scientific prerequisites[1, 2, 22]; Connect neuronal/nerve structure, axonal cargo transport and injury classification before the detailed excitability chapter.
- **24. Membrane excitability and synaptic transmission:** lectures[56, 57]; scientific prerequisites[2, 22, 23]; Develop resting potential, graded integration and action-potential mechanisms before explaining synaptic transmission.
- **25. Glia, sensory systems and neurological dysfunction:** lectures[58, 59, 60]; scientific prerequisites[2, 22, 23, 24]; Explain neurovascular/support-cell mechanisms, all source sensory modalities and named neurological disease mechanisms.
