Reading the compartments

a · Cortex. Purple endothelial cells line the white lumen L. Teal P identifies a pericyte; cyan A identifies an astrocyte endfoot. The white arrow in inset2 identifies an endothelial tight junction. The main image has a 1 µm bar.

b–c · Median eminence (ME). This specialised region differs from cortex. The source marks fibroblasts red, extracellular matrix ECM and endothelial fenestrations with white arrowheads. These examples must not be presented as ordinary sealed cortical capillaries.

d · Different optical evidence. CD31 labels vessels; AQP4 labels an astrocyte-associated water channel. This fluorescence comparison is not an electron micrograph. Its source bar is 20 µm.

The study used mice aged 8–14 weeks. TEM tissue was perfusion-fixed, post-fixed, osmicated, resin-embedded and cut into 80 nm sections. It is recorded prepared tissue rather than living natural colour. Sex and strain of this particular displayed capillary are not assigned here.

Relative compartments and junction morphology can be inspected. A still image does not measure glucose flux, permeability, transporter kinetics or live opening of junctions. Basement membrane composition and specific transporter identity are not independently resolved by these labels.
