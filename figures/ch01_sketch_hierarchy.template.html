Chapter 1 · Structure and function

Organization across scales

One physiological question, four levels of organization. Use a whole-body sketch, then observed anatomy from the skin surface to a stained tissue section and a matrix-producing cell.

One illustrative body sketch and three independent photographs/micrographs. They are not successive fields from the same person or a registered microscope zoom. Only the histology field has an archive-supplied physical width. Histology and fluorescence colors reflect staining.

Body / organism

01 / 04

What to identify

The whole individual: head, trunk and upper and lower limbs. Clothing covers the trunk; no internal organs are depicted.

The physiological question

How do organ systems cooperate to maintain the environment in which cells function?

The organism is the level at which separate organs must coordinate. This sketch introduces the whole-body frame of reference; the following images are observations of tissue and cells.

Illustrative anatomy, not an observed person, population template or calibrated measurement. Palms face forward and thumbs point laterally.

Organ / skin surface

02 / 04

What to identify

The exposed skin surface: fine furrows, hairs and natural variation in surface relief. Individual epidermal cells and the deeper dermis are not resolved.

The physiological question

How can one organ form a boundary between the internal and external environments?

This is the surface of skin, an organ made from multiple tissues. To see their arrangement, the next image looks at a physical tissue section.

Body site and physical field width were not supplied by the photographer. No numerical magnification is claimed.

Tissue / skin section

03 / 04

What to identify

A superficial epithelial band borders deeper fibrous connective tissue. The owning record identifies elastic fibres as purple and collagen as green to yellow.

The physiological question

How do cells and their extracellular matrix work together within a tissue?

The visible fibrous matrix is part of the tissue, not empty space between cells. A section reveals arrangement that the surface photograph cannot show.

The archive states that the complete horizontal field is 400 μm. Section thickness, body site and donor are unspecified.

Cell / human fibroblast

04 / 04

What to identify

The yellow signal marks the nucleus; red signal marks mitochondria. The fluorescent image does not resolve every organelle or demonstrate membrane transport.

The physiological question

How do distinct intracellular structures support the work of a cell?

Fibroblasts produce extracellular matrix, including collagen. This separate cell image links a matrix-producing cell type to the tissue-level question.

No physical calibration supplied. Red and yellow are stain signals, not the natural colors of living organelles.
